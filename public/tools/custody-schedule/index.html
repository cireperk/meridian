How the common custody schedules work

2-2-3

One parent has the children for two days, the other for two, then the first has a three-day stretch. The next week it flips, so each parent gets a long weekend every other week. Children never go more than three days without seeing either parent.

2-2-5-5

Each parent has two weekdays, then five-day blocks that cover the weekend on alternating weeks. The weekly rhythm stays the same, which many families find easier to remember than 2-2-3.

Week on / week off

The children live with one parent for seven days, then the other. There is one handoff a week. It suits older children and parents who live close enough to manage a longer gap.

Every other weekend

One parent has the children on weekdays, and the other has alternating weekends. Many orders add a midweek dinner or extra holiday time on top of this base pattern.

Does this replace my court order?

No. This is a planning tool. Your court order or parenting plan controls holidays, summers, and exceptions, so use the calendar as a starting point and confirm it matches your agreement.
